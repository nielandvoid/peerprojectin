The Peer Project // Mentor Guide

Welcome to The Peer Project. You are the backbone of this initiative. By volunteering your time, you are helping bridge the gap between confusion and clarity for students who are walking the path you just finished.

This guide is designed to give you a framework for how to approach your mentorship, handle common situations, and make the most out of your pairing.

Core Principles

  • You are a Guide, Not a Teacher: Your job isn't to teach them thermodynamics or write their English essays. Your job is to help them figure out how to study, where to find resources, and what strategy works best.
  • Listen First, Advise Second: Before prescribing a solution, understand their specific context. A student failing because of procrastination needs different advice than a student failing because their basics are weak.
  • Keep it Real: Don't sugarcoat things, but don't be overly harsh either. Share your own struggles and failures - it makes you relatable and builds trust.
  • Maintain Boundaries: You are volunteering your time. Set clear expectations about when you are available to reply and how quickly they can expect a response.

The First Check-In

Your first interaction sets the tone for the entire mentorship.

Goals of the first meeting:

  1. Break the Ice: Get to know them beyond their academics. What are their hobbies? Why did they join TPP?
  2. Understand the Baseline: Where are they currently at? What are their biggest pain points (e.g., time management, specific subjects, lack of motivation)?
  3. Set Expectations: Agree on how often you will communicate. (e.g., "I'll check in every Wednesday and Sunday, but feel free to drop questions anytime.")
Sample First Message:
"Hey! I'm [Your Pseudonym], your mentor for the next few months. I was let know that you're struggling with [Issue]. I actually dealt with the exact same thing when I was in your shoes. Before we dive into academics, tell me a bit about yourself - what do you do when you're not studying?"

Structuring Ongoing Sessions

Since TPP relies on asynchronous text check-ins on Discord, you need a loose structure to keep things moving.

  • The Weekly Sync: Pick 1-2 days a week to do a deeper check-in. Ask specific questions:
    What did you accomplish this week?
    What blocked you?
    What's the goal for next week?
  • Actionable Advice: Break down big goals into small, actionable steps. Instead of saying "Study more chemistry," say "Try to complete 20 numericals from Chapter 3 by Friday."
  • Accountability: Follow up on the goals you set together. If they didn't meet them, ask why (without judgment) and adjust the plan.

Handling Common Challenges

  • The Unresponsive Mentee: If they ghost you, don't take it personally. Send a gentle nudge: "Hey, just checking in! Let me know if you need any help this week." If they remain unresponsive for over two weeks, let the admin team know.
  • The Overwhelmed Mentee: If they are stressed, help them prioritize. Tell them to drop the complex stuff and focus entirely on the basics until they regain their footing.
  • The "Tell Me Exactly What to Do" Mentee: Resist the urge to give them a rigid timetable. Ask them questions that lead them to build their own schedule. Ownership is key.

Logistics & Discord Etiquette

  • Privacy: Keep conversations within your private #mentor-mentee channel. Do not share your real name, personal social media, or phone number.
  • Asynchronous Communication: You are not expected to reply instantly. Reply when you have the time to give a thoughtful answer.
  • Escalation: If a mentee shares concerning information regarding their mental health or well-being, notify the admin team immediately. Do not try to handle crisis situations alone.

Support for Mentors

You are not alone in this!

  • The Mentors' Lounge: Use the #mentors-lounge channel to bounce ideas off other mentors, share resources, or vent.
  • Ask the Experts: We have experienced educators (like @iteachchem) available to help. If you're stuck on how to handle a specific mentoring challenge, don't hesitate to ask for advice.

Thank you again for stepping up. You are about to make a massive difference in someone's academic journey. Let's get to work!
